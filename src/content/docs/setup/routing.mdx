With a 2-in, 2-out interface there is nothing to set: the guitar goes into input 1 and outputs 1
and 2 play the effects in stereo. An interface with more inputs and outputs gets them all, and
the **Routing** page says what each one does:

- play a dry guitar, or the signal before the amp, on its own output, to reamp later or for the
  front of house;
- feed a second instrument (a bass DI, a synth) into the chain;
- send a different mix to the in-ear monitors.

Open it from **⋮** → **Routing**. A line at the top sums it up, for instance *2 inputs and 4
outputs. Effects on input 1, main mix on outputs 1, 2.* Changes are heard at once and kept; there
is nothing to save. On a 480-pixel-high screen, scroll down to see the sends and returns of the
preset. The back button at the top left, or a hold on the encoder, goes back to the page you
opened it from.

## Inputs

<Shot src={outputs} alt="The Routing page with 4 inputs and 4 outputs: outputs 1-2 play the main mix, output 3 plays Send 1 at -3 dB, output 4 plays Input 2" caption="A 4-in, 4-out interface: the main mix on 1 and 2, the signal before the amp on 3, the bass DI as it comes in on 4." />

**Effects input** chooses the input that goes through the effects and the tuner, input 1 by
default. Each input has its own meter. The other inputs are not processed, but they can be played
on an output or brought into the chain by a [Return block](#send-and-return-blocks).

## Outputs

Each output, or pair of outputs, has a card:

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Source** | **Off**, the **Main mix** of the effects, **Send 1** to **Send 4**, or an **Input** played as it comes in, without effects |
| **Stereo / mono** (the button next to the name) | Joins an output and the next one into a stereo pair, or splits a pair into two outputs |
| **Level** | −60 to +12 dB |
| **Balance** | Left / right balance of a stereo pair |
| **L+R / L / R** | On a single output playing a stereo source: both sides summed, or one side only |
| **M** | Mute |
| **S** | Solo: while an output is soloed, only soloed outputs play |
| **Ø** | Flips the polarity, for a mic'd cabinet out of phase with the direct signal |
| **EQ** | Opens the [output EQ](#output-eq-and-limiter). Lit while it is on |
| **LIMIT** | Opens the [limiter](#output-eq-and-limiter). Lit while it is on |
| **GR** | How much the limiter takes off, in dB |

Level, mute and source changes are ramped over 10 ms, so they never click. When the tuner's
**Mute while tuning** is on, the effects and the inputs played straight through are muted while
it is open.

The outputs belong to the rig, not to a preset: changing presets never changes the levels sent
to the front of house. They are kept in `routing.json` on the data partition.

## Output EQ and limiter

Each output or stereo pair has its own EQ and limiter. They fit the rig to the system it plays
on, a PA, a pair of monitors or headphones, whatever the preset. Like the rest of the output,
they are kept for the rig, not for a preset. Tap **EQ** or **LIMIT** on an output card to open
them; **✕** closes the panel.

### EQ

<Shot src={eq} alt="The EQ of outputs 1-2: a curve with the points LC, LS, 1, 2, HS and HC, mid 2 boosted around 1.5 kHz, the high cut at 10 kHz; under it the bands Low cut, Low, Mid 1, Mid 2, High, High cut, with Low selected and its Freq and Gain knobs" caption="The output EQ: drag a point on the curve, or pick a band and turn its knobs." />

Six bands, from low to high:

| Band | Controls | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Low cut** | **Freq** | Removes the lows under the frequency, to clean up a boomy stage |
| **Low** | **Freq**, **Gain** | Raises or lowers everything under the frequency |
| **Mid 1**, **Mid 2** | **Freq**, **Gain**, **Q** | Raises or lowers a band around the frequency. A higher Q makes it narrower |
| **High** | **Freq**, **Gain** | Raises or lowers everything above the frequency |
| **High cut** | **Freq** | Removes the highs above the frequency, for a harsh PA or a full-range speaker |

Drag a point on the curve to move its band: left and right for the frequency, up and down for
the gain. Dragging a point turns its band and the EQ on. A double tap on a point turns its band
on or off. **ON** turns the whole EQ on or off, and **FLAT** puts every band back to its default: flat, with both cuts off. The
gains go from −12 to +12 dB. The EQ is off on a new output.

### Limiter

<Shot src={limiter} alt="The limiter of outputs 1-2: the Ceiling knob at -1.0 dB, the Release knob at 100 ms, and a gain reduction readout of -5.1 dB with its meter" caption="The limiter keeps the output under the ceiling and shows how much it takes off." />

The limiter keeps every peak of the output under the **Ceiling**, so the mixer, the speaker or
the recorder after it never clips, even with a loud preset or a big boost. It is on by default,
at −1 dB.

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Ceiling** | The highest level the output reaches, −12 to 0 dB. Set it a little under where the next device clips |
| **Release** | How fast the level comes back after a peak, 10 ms to 1 s, 100 ms by default |

The **GR** readout shows how much the limiter takes off. A few dB on the loudest notes is fine; if
it takes off a lot all the time, lower the output level or the preset instead.

The limiter looks 1 ms ahead to catch each peak before it plays. That delays the output by 1 ms
while the limiter is on; an output with the limiter off is not delayed.

## Send and return blocks

<Shot src={rack} alt="The rack of the Guitar and bass preset: Pedal, a Send 1 block, an A/B split with Amp and Cabinet on A and an Input 2 return block then a Cabinet on B, then Space" caption="Send 1 after the pedal; on branch B of the split, a return brings in input 2 before the bass cabinet." />

Two blocks of the rack, added from **+** like any other (family **Routing**, at the end of the
list), take the signal out of the chain or bring an input in, wherever they are placed:

| Block | Controls | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Send** | **Send** (1 to 4), **Level** | Copies the signal at its place to a send, while the signal carries on through the chain. An output whose source is that send plays it. |
| **Return** | **Input**, **Level**, **Mix** | Brings an input of the interface into the chain. At 100 % mix, the default, the input replaces the signal: the blocks before it are no longer heard on that path. A lower mix blends the input in. |

The rack shows them as small jack plates named after their send or input: **Send 1**,
**Input 2**. They are saved with the preset and can be bypassed or changed by
[scenes](../../using/live/), like any block: a bypassed send stops sending, a bypassed return
lets the signal through. Changes are ramped, so they never click.

<Shot src={blocks} alt="The Sends and returns card of the Routing page: Send 1 after Pedal, Return Input 2 at the start of branch B of the A/B split" caption="The Routing page lists the send and return blocks of the preset, to see what feeds each send." />

A send placed in a branch of an A/B split is only heard while that branch is selected. During a
preset change, the sends crossfade with the rest of the sound.

## Examples

### Dry signal for reamping

1. Add a **Send** block just before the amp, set to **Send 1**.
2. Set an output to **Send 1**.

That output now plays the guitar with its pedals but without the amp, ready to be recorded and
reamped. Put the send at the start of the chain for a fully dry signal, or set the output to the
guitar's input instead.

### Guitar and bass on one pedalboard

1. Plug the bass into input 2.
2. Add an [A/B split](../../using/rack/#splits): the amp and its cabinet on branch A, a bass
   cabinet on branch B.
3. Add a **Return** block at the start of branch B, set to **Input 2**.

Switching the split to B, from a scene, a footswitch or MIDI, plays the bass through its cabinet
and the blocks after the split. The branch that is not selected is not processed and costs
nothing.

### A reverb on its own output

Put the reverb alone in one branch of a [Parallel mix](../../using/rack/#splits) split, turn its
own mix to 100 % wet, and add a **Send** block after it, set to **Send 2**. The main mix still
plays the guitar with the reverb, and an output set to **Send 2** plays the reverb alone: for a
wet / dry / wet rig, or a separate reverb channel at the desk.

## Limits

- At most 16 inputs, 16 outputs and 4 sends.
- The [looper](../../using/tuner-looper/#looper) only records and plays the main mix.
- A preset made on a larger interface keeps its returns on inputs that are missing; those
  returns play silence until that interface is back.
