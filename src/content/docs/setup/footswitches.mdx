On a Raspberry Pi, up to 8 footswitches and a rotary encoder can be wired straight to the GPIO
header. No extra board, no resistor: each input is wired between its GPIO and ground, and the
Pi's internal pull-ups do the rest.

:::tip
Any MIDI foot controller works too, with nothing to wire: see [MIDI](../../using/midi/).
:::

## Parts

- Momentary footswitches (SPST, normally open), the kind used for tap tempo.
- Optionally, a rotary encoder with a push button (the common EC11 type).
- Wires to the Pi's 40-pin header, ideally through a connector so the box can be opened.

## Default wiring

| Input | GPIO (BCM) | Header pin |
|---|---|---|
| Footswitch 1 | 17 | 11 |
| Footswitch 2 | 27 | 13 |
| Footswitch 3 | 22 | 15 |
| Footswitch 4 | 23 | 16 |
| Encoder A | 5 | 29 |
| Encoder B | 6 | 31 |
| Encoder button | 13 | 33 |
| Ground | | 6, 9, 14, 20, 25, 30, 34, 39 |

Wire the other leg of each switch, and the encoder's common pin, to a ground pin.

:::caution[With an audio HAT]
Do not wire switches to the GPIOs an audio HAT uses. 2-channel I2S HATs take GPIO 18 to 21.
8-channel HATs (HiFiBerry DAC8x, RaspiAudio 8x8) take GPIO 18 to 27, and GPIO 5 to detect their
inputs: they overlap footswitches 2 to 4 and encoder A of the default wiring. Pick other GPIOs
on the [Hardware page](#settings--hardware): with an 8-channel HAT, GPIO 4, 6, 12, 13, 16 and
17 stay free (7 to 11 too when SPI is off).

OpenRig asks the kernel which GPIOs a driver holds and leaves those inputs out, rather than
cutting the HAT off its pins. The Hardware page says which, for instance
*FS 2: GPIO 27 is used by the audio HAT (I2S), left out*.
:::

## Settings → Hardware

**⋮** → **Settings** → **Hardware**, the last card of Settings, opens the Hardware page. It sets
what is wired:

<Shot src={hw} alt="The Hardware page: 4 footswitches on GPIO 17, 27, 22 and 23, each with a LED, and Tap tempo on FS 4 turned on" caption="The Hardware page: four footswitches, the last one tapping the tempo." />

- **Footswitches**: from none to 8, and the GPIO of each. A LED next to each one lights while it is
  pressed, to check the wiring.
- **Tap tempo on the last footswitch**: on by default with two switches or more. The last switch
  then taps the tempo, and held, opens the tuner.
- **Encoder**: its A and B GPIOs, its push button or none, and its steps per detent. Lower the
  steps if it takes several clicks to move.

Changes apply at once and are kept on the data partition. **‹ Settings** at the top left, or a
hold on the encoder, goes back to Settings.

Without GPIO (the desktop app, a mini PC), the card reads **Footswitches and tap tempo**: it still
sets how many footswitches the keys `1` to `8` play, and the tap tempo.

## What they do

The footswitches follow the mode of the [Live page](../../using/live/): blocks on and off in
Stomp mode, scenes in Scenes mode, presets in Setlist mode. They act when pressed.

## The encoder

The encoder drives the whole UI without the touch screen. **Turn** to move, **press** to open or
adjust, **hold** (0.6 s) to go back. A frame shows what is focused; it gets thicker while you
adjust a value. On a USB keyboard, the arrows turn, `Return` presses and `Escape` holds.

| Page | Turn | Press | Hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Rack](../../using/rack/) | Select a block; past the last one, the **+** button (an empty rack starts there) | Open the block's editor, or on **+** the Add effect page | Page switcher |
| [Block editor](../../using/editor/#with-the-encoder) | Go through the controls, Arrange, On and the bin | Adjust the control | Back to the rack |
| [Add effect](../../using/rack/#adding-a-block) | Go through the list | Add the block | Back to the rack |
| [Presets](../../using/presets/) | Pick a preset | Load it | Page switcher |
| [Live](../../using/live/#with-the-encoder) | The switches, then the tempo, tap and the mode | Act like the footswitch, or adjust the tempo | Page switcher |
| [Looper](../../using/tuner-looper/#looper) | Record, Overdub, Play, Stop, Undo, Clear, Loop ×2, ½ Speed, Loops, then the tempo, TAP, the bar, Count-in, Quantize, Click, the click level and the level | Trigger it, or adjust a value | Page switcher |
| [Tuner](../../using/tuner-looper/#tuner) | Mute and the reference pitch | Toggle mute, or adjust the pitch | Page switcher |
| [Settings](../settings/) | Every control, card by card from the top | What a tap does, or adjust a value | Page switcher |
| [Hardware](#settings--hardware) | The footswitches, tap tempo and knob encoders | What a tap does, or adjust a value | Back to Settings |

### Adjusting a value

A block's control, the tempo, the looper's values, the tuner's reference pitch and the steppers
and segments of Settings work the same way:

1. Press to start adjusting.
2. Turn to change the value.
3. Press to keep it, or hold to go back to the value before.

A switch flips at once on a press.

### Page switcher

Holding on a main page opens the page switcher: Rack, Presets, Live, Tuner, Looper and Settings.
Turn to pick a page, press to open it. Tapping a page works too.

Hold also goes back one level elsewhere: it closes the **⋮** menu, the power and update dialogs and
the on-screen keyboard, leaves the store, the setlists and the Routing page. While a dialog is
open, the encoder never acts on the page behind it.

### Settings with the encoder

Since OpenRig 0.14.0 the encoder reaches every control of [Settings](../settings/) and of the
Hardware page, card by card from the top:

- A **press** does what a tap does: flips a switch, taps a button, opens **Hardware ›**.
- A **stepper** (sample rate, buffer, DSP cores…) or a **segmented** control (screen rotation, the
  hotspot): press, turn to change it, press again to stop.
- A **list** (Interface, Wi-Fi country): press to open it, turn to move, press to pick, hold to
  close it.
- **Audio & system**: after a change, turn on to **Cancel** or **Apply**.
- **Wi-Fi**: **Scan**, the switch, then the networks. A press on a saved network shows
  **Connect** or **Disconnect** and **Forget**, which the next turns reach (hold closes them); on
  a new secured network it opens the password keyboard.
- **Network MIDI**: **Allow** and **Block** of each computer connected, **Remove** of each device
  listed.
- **TONE3000 Connect**: **Disconnect** stops first on **Cancel**, then on the **Disconnect** that
  confirms.
- A text field (a password, the session name) opens the on-screen keyboard: type on the screen or
  a USB keyboard; a hold cancels it.

On the Hardware page the encoder skips its own settings (on / off, A and B pins, push button and
its GPIO, steps per detent): changed with the encoder, they would apply at once and cut it off.
Set them by touch.

## Knob encoders (I2C)

Encoder boards on the I2C bus give the rack's [knob strip](../../using/rack/#quick-knobs) real
knobs, one encoder per field:

- Adafruit I2C rotary encoders (seesaw, addresses 0x36 to 0x3D);
- the Adafruit Quad rotary encoder (0x49 to 0x50);
- the M5Stack 8Encoder (0x41).

Wire SDA to GPIO 2 (pin 3), SCL to GPIO 3 (pin 5), and power and ground. Several boards of the
same kind chain on the bus, and their encoders count in address order. The image turns the I2C
bus on.

| Encoder | Turn | Press | Hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 to 5, knob | Change the value | Swap with the value before the turns (A/B) | Back to the default |
| 1 to 5, switch | Flip it | Flip it | Back to the default |
| 1 to 5, list | Step through it; open, move through it | Open it; open, pick | Back to the default; open, close it |
| 1 to 5, presets or model | Open the list, then move through it | Open the list, then pick | Close the list |
| 6 | The strip's pages | | |

The [Hardware page](#settings--hardware) sets the board, the counts per detent and the direction,
and says which addresses answered. Without boards, the keys `a s d f g h` turn encoders 1 to 6
(with Shift, the other way) and `z x c v b n` press them (with Shift, hold), on the rack page.
